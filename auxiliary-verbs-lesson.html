01

Learn: Auxiliary Verbs

Auxiliary verbs work together with main verbs. The most common auxiliary verbs are do, have, and be.

Use do

with negative statements and questions and with affirmative and negative short answers.

She doesn't like social networking websites.
Do you send lots of text messages? Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
Did Thomas answer your email? Yes, he did. / No, he didn't.

Use have

with present perfect and past perfect affirmative and negative statements, questions, and short answers.

She has (not) created her own website.
Has she posted any photos yet? Yes, she has. / No, she hasn't.
Have you charged your cell phone? Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.
I had gotten his email an hour before he called me.

Use be

with progressive affirmative and negative statements, questions, and short answers.

She is (not) answering her cell phone.
Is your cell phone working? Yes, it is. / No, it isn't.
He was using his brother's cell phone yesterday.
Were you surfing the Internet last night? Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't.

Also use be

with passive affirmative and negative statements, questions, and short answers.

The Internet is (not) used by millions of people.
Were you given a password? Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't.

Example: I am ready. I was late. I do my homework. I did it yesterday. I have finished. — but he / she / it takes is, does, has instead.

Six Structures, Three Forms Each

Simple Present — do / does

Affirmative
Subject + verb (base or -s)
She checks her email every day.
Negative
Subject + do/does + not + verb (base)
She does not check her email on weekends.
Question
Do/Does + subject + verb (base)?
Does she check her email every day?

Simple Past — did

Affirmative
Subject + verb (past form)
He sent the message.
Negative
Subject + did + not + verb (base)
He did not send the message.
Question
Did + subject + verb (base)?
Did he send the message?

Present Perfect — have / has

Affirmative
Subject + have/has + past participle
I have finished my homework.
Negative
Subject + have/has + not + past participle
I have not finished my homework.
Question
Have/Has + subject + past participle?
Have you finished your homework?

Past Perfect — had

Affirmative
Subject + had + past participle
She had left before I arrived.
Negative
Subject + had + not + past participle
She had not left before I arrived.
Question
Had + subject + past participle?
Had she left before you arrived?

Progressive — am / is / are / was / were + verb-ing

Affirmative
Subject + am/is/are/was/were + verb-ing
I am studying right now.
Negative
Subject + am/is/are/was/were + not + verb-ing
I am not studying right now.
Question
Am/Is/Are/Was/Were + subject + verb-ing?
Are you studying right now?

Passive — am / is / are / was / were + past participle

Affirmative
Subject + am/is/are/was/were + past participle
The email is sent every day.
Negative
Subject + am/is/are/was/were + not + past participle
The email is not sent on weekends.
Question
Am/Is/Are/Was/Were + subject + past participle?
Is the email sent every day?
